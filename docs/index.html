CRKL-Space

Bringing the stars to you!

Our Mission

CRKL (“Circle”) is a software company dedicated to bridging the gap between the public and space science. We believe everyone—not just experts—deserves to understand the world and universe around us. Our goal is to make space information accessible, simple, and easy to understand.

How CRKL-Space Aids In Our Mission

Simplifying Information

Displaying simplified information will allow users to get the basic idea of missions, launches, and spacecrafts without information overload.

Centralizing Data

Cultivating an environment where all space related information is held will reduce research time.

Organizing Data

An efficient database where data is organized.

Accessibility

Our main goal is to make space science accessible for people without an expert background.

The Real-World Problem

Finding research or general information about space exploration online is often difficult. Space agencies such as NASA and ESA publish many complex datasets scattered across the web. This makes independent research harder: aspiring researchers and hobbyists don't know where to look, or get lost in the amount of information. CRKL-Space brings this widely dispersed information to the public through one simplified, condensed database, where anyone can search for space agencies, their missions, their spacecraft, and their launches.

Our Project's Value

CRKL-Space gives the public, researchers, and anyone with an interest in space a quick way to find information about space exploration. It can be challenging for people who are new to space science to know what information is out there. Instead of searching across many websites, users find related missions, agencies, spacecraft, and launches in one place, in a simplified format, which removes a time-consuming research process.

Future: From Class Project to Product

CRKL-Space could become a real software product, because its scope can grow much larger. For now we cover a few research topics for the sake of time, but many more fields within space science could be integrated into our database. Our next steps are importing real data from the Launch Library 2 API and displaying images. A publicly available version would also let users create accounts, save information to their accounts, and contribute comments and links.
